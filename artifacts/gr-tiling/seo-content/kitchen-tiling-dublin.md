## INLINE PROSE SECTION 1

### Kitchen tiling in Dublin, from splashback to full floor

A kitchen is the hardest-working room in the house, and the tiling has to earn its keep. Behind the hob it takes spitting fat and heat; around the sink it takes constant splashing; underfoot it takes dropped pans, dog claws and muddy boots off the back garden. We tile Dublin kitchens so the finish still looks sharp years after the paint elsewhere has tired — the right tile in the right place, set out so the eye lands on full tiles and clean joints, not awkward slivers next to the extractor.

Most of what we do here is splashbacks and the wall-and-floor work that surrounds a fitted kitchen. A **metro splashback Dublin** homeowners ask for by name — a classic 100x200mm brick bond, or the same tile turned vertical or laid in a **herringbone splashback** for something with more movement — sits behind the hob and along the worktop run. We also fit patterned and encaustic-look tiles for a feature run, mosaic strips, and larger-format wall tiles where a client wants fewer grout lines. The craft is in the setting-out and the cuts: tiling around kitchen units means working to the underside of wall cabinets, meeting the worktop with a consistent joint, and cutting cleanly around sockets, switches and the boxing for extractor ducting.

Grout choice matters more in a kitchen than anywhere else in the house. Standard cement grout is porous — it drinks in grease, tomato sauce and coffee, then stains and darkens along the joints above the hob. For splashbacks and worktop returns we recommend and fit **epoxy or a high-performance stain-resistant grout** where it counts, so a wipe with a cloth actually cleans it rather than smearing it. Where tile meets worktop, sink or the run of units, that junction is sealed with a sanitary-grade silicone, not grout — it flexes as the kitchen moves and timber worktops breathe, and it stays mould-free far longer.

- Stain-resistant epoxy grout offered where grease and heat are worst

### Tiling around cabinets, appliances and the messy real world of a kitchen

No two Dublin kitchens are set out the same, and a splashback is only as good as the cuts around everything bolted to the wall. Sockets sit at odd heights above the worktop; the extractor hood and its ducting eat into the top of the run; a boiler, a boxed-in pipe or a window reveal interrupts the field. We measure and cut so tile lines run through these obstructions cleanly — sockets sitting square on a grout joint or centred on a tile, not clipped at a random angle — because that is what separates a fitted splashback from a DIY one. Where an appliance like a range cooker or American fridge sits proud, we tile behind and to the reveal so there are no bare patches when it's pulled out.

For kitchen and utility floors we fit dense **porcelain** — very low water absorption, frost-tolerant near back doors, and genuinely easy to keep clean, which is what most people actually want underfoot in a kitchen. ## OPTIONAL DEEP SECTION

### Why kitchen grout goes grey above the hob — and how we stop it

The single most common complaint we hear about older kitchen tiling is grey, greasy grout lines above the cooker that no amount of scrubbing brings back. It is not dirt sitting on the surface — it is grease absorbed *into* a porous cement grout. Standard cementitious grout is a sponge: fine for a spare-room wall, poor above a Dublin hob that fries a fry-up most weekends. Once fat and cooking oils soak in, they oxidise and darken, and bleach only lightens the very top layer before it wicks back.

The fix is specification, not elbow grease. In the hardest-hit zones — the hob splashback, the run behind the sink — we fit an **epoxy or high-performance grout** that is effectively non-porous. It resists grease, wine, turmeric and coffee, wipes clean with a damp cloth, and needs no sealing or annual renewal. It costs more and it sets fast, so it demands a tiler who works cleanly and to a plan; done right, it is the difference between a splashback that still looks new in ten years and one that looks tired in three. For the rest of a kitchen wall, a good stain-resistant cement grout is often perfectly sensible — we match the grout to the exposure rather than blanket-spec the dearest option everywhere.

Two more details do the quiet work. First, **movement junctions get silicone, not grout**: where tiles meet a worktop, a sink, an internal corner or a run of units, the assembly flexes as timber and worktops expand — rigid grout in that gap cracks and lets water track behind, so we cut those lines in with a sanitary-grade anti-mould silicone that stays flexible and clean. Second, the substrate has to be sound and primed before a tile goes up; tiling a splashback straight onto dusty or unsealed plaster is a hidden cause of tiles debonding later. If your existing kitchen tiling is sound but the grout has simply stained or cracked, you often don't need a re-tile at all — raking out and regrouting restores it for far less, which is our [tile & grout repairs](/services/tile-repairs-dublin) work rather than a full new splashback.

## FAQ (7 Q&A)

**Q1. How much does a kitchen splashback cost in Dublin?**
There's no single figure — it depends on the length of the run, the tile, and whether you want a straightforward brick-bond metro or a herringbone or patterned feature that takes far more cutting and labour. Small splashbacks are a modest job; a full wraparound with premium tile and epoxy grout is more. We always give an honest, written quote after seeing the kitchen, and you can read more about [what a splashback costs](/cost-guide) before you call.

**Q2. What's the difference between a metro and a herringbone splashback?**
A metro splashback is the classic rectangular "brick" tile, usually 100x200mm, laid in a horizontal offset bond — clean, timeless and quick to set out. Herringbone uses the same or a slimmer tile turned to a 45 or 90-degree zig-zag, which adds movement and a more crafted look but needs far more cutting at the ends of each row. Herringbone typically adds noticeably to the labour; both look superb behind a hob.

**Q3. Can you tile around my existing kitchen units and worktops?**
Yes — that's the core of kitchen tiling. We work to the underside of wall cabinets, meet the worktop with a consistent joint, and cut cleanly around sockets, switches, taps and appliance gaps. Where tile meets the worktop or units we finish with a flexible silicone rather than rigid grout, so the joint copes with movement and stays watertight. Neat cuts around obstructions are exactly what separates a fitted splashback from a DIY one.

**Q4. What grout should I use in a kitchen so it doesn't go grey?**
Above a hob and around the sink we recommend an epoxy or high-performance stain-resistant grout, which is effectively non-porous — it shrugs off grease, wine and coffee and wipes clean without sealing. Standard cement grout is porous and absorbs cooking grease, which is why older splashbacks go grey along the joints. For lower-exposure walls a good stain-resistant cement grout is often fine. We match the grout to the zone rather than over-spec everywhere.

**Q5. Do you tile kitchen and utility floors as well as splashbacks?**
Yes. We fit dense porcelain floors for kitchens and utility rooms — low water absorption, hard-wearing, and genuinely easy to mop clean. Utility rooms also get a tiled splash zone behind the sink and washing machine. If your job is mainly about levelling an uneven period-house floor or large-format technique across an open-plan space, that sits under our [floor & wall prep and large-format tiling](/services/floor-wall-tiling-dublin) service, and we'll tell you which fits.

**Q6. My splashback tiles are fine but the grout looks awful — do I need a new splashback?**
Usually not. If the tiles are sound and well-bonded but the grout is stained, cracked or mouldy, raking it out and regrouting — plus renewing the silicone at the worktop and corners — restores the splashback at a fraction of the cost of re-tiling. A full re-tile only makes sense if tiles are drummy, lifting or cracked. That refresh work is our tile and grout repairs service rather than new kitchen tiling.
