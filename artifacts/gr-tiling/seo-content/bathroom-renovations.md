### One contractor for the whole bathroom, not five numbers to chase

A full bathroom renovation is one of the few jobs in a Dublin home that touches nearly every trade: a plumber for first and second fix, an electrician for the extract fan and RCD-protected circuits, a plasterer, a waterproofer and a tiler, plus the strip-out and skip. Manage that yourself and you become an unpaid project manager, standing in the gap when the tiler arrives before the plumbing is pressure-tested, or when the sanitaryware turns up a week after the trades have moved on. Sequencing is where bathrooms go wrong, and it goes wrong when nobody owns the whole timeline.

That is what "on time, on budget" actually means in practice: a realistic programme agreed up front, one honest written quote rather than a tempting online figure that balloons on week two, and no surprise change-order every time a trade hands off to the next. ## INLINE PROSE SECTION 2

### What a full bathroom renovation actually includes

"Renovation" gets used loosely, so here is exactly what a full refit with us covers. First, the **strip-out and disposal** — the old suite, tiles, and any tired boarding come out, and the waste leaves in our skip, not your driveway. Dublin's older housing stock often has a surprise or two underneath: on strip-out we frequently find damaged subfloors, perished lead or steel pipework, damp behind the old tiles, or a return-room wall that is out of plumb by 20mm or more. We flag anything hidden in writing before we press on, so the scope stays honest rather than creeping quietly.

Next comes the **first fix and preparation** — any new pipe runs and cabling, the extract fan and lighting circuits coordinated with the electrician, stud alterations, floor stiffening where a bouncy suspended timber floor would otherwise crack tile, plastering and floor levelling, and tile backer board where it belongs. Then **waterproofing** in the wet zones, **tiling** set out from the most visible line so no sliver cuts land at eye level, **second fix** to connect the suite, and the **fit-out**: sanitaryware, shower screen, brassware, accessories. Finally the **finish** — grouting, anti-mould sanitary silicone at every movement junction, sealing where needed, and a full snag before we hand back.

The deep tiling craft — patterns, large-format, detailed setting-out — is covered across [all our tiling services](/services/tiling-services), and what shapes the price lives on our cost guide. On this page we stay on the whole-project picture, but it is worth knowing what shifts a quote: keeping the existing layout keeps it down, while moving the toilet or soil stack, discovering damp or rot, or specifying a wet-room build-up pushes it up. For the full picture, see [what shapes a bathroom renovation quote in Dublin](/cost-guide).

## OPTIONAL DEEP SECTION

### Why renovation sequencing is the whole game in a Dublin bathroom

Most bathroom disasters are not bad tiling or a leaking joint in isolation — they are a sequencing failure, where two stages happened in the wrong order and one destroyed the other. The classic is tiling before the first fix is finished and pressure-tested: a fortnight later a slow weep shows up behind the wall, and now the fix means smashing new tile to reach a pipe. Just as common is tanking that was tiled over before it had cured, or an extract fan and lighting left as an afterthought so the electrician is chasing walls that were already boarded and skimmed. Ventilation and RCD-protected circuits are the electrician's work, but they only land cleanly if they are coordinated *around* the tiling, not bolted on after.

The order that works is not negotiable and it is why one contractor matters. Survey and design first, layout signed off with you, then strip-out. First-fix plumbing and electrics roughed in and pressure-tested. Structural and substrate works next — stud alterations, stiffening a suspended timber floor, backer boards — because a deck that flexes underfoot will crack rigid tile and grout no matter how good the tiler is. Only then waterproofing, and only once it has cured, tiling. Second fix, sanitaryware and fit-out follow, and grouting, silicone and sealing close it out. Miss the pressure test, rush the cure, or let the sanitaryware arrive before the room is ready, and the programme unravels.

Dublin's housing makes this discipline matter more, not less. Victorian and Edwardian returns in Rathmines or Phibsborough sit on bouncy timber joists and walls out of plumb; 1930s–70s semis in Crumlin or Raheny hide timber-stud partitions where a heavy mirror or rail needs a pattress fitted before the board goes on; even newer apartments have floating chipboard decks that move. None of that is a problem when the person planning the job knows what is behind the old tiles and books the stages to suit. It becomes an expensive problem when five separate trades each assume someone else checked.

For a standard like-for-like bathroom, a renovation that does not extend the house or alter its use is generally **exempted development** — no planning permission required — though we always confirm your specifics rather than assume. What you should insist on from any fitter is the boring stuff the cheapest quote quietly skips: pressure testing, proper tanking that is allowed to cure, floor stiffening, priming, movement joints and pattressing. It costs a little more on day one and far less over the fifteen-plus years you will use the room.

## FAQ (6-8)

**Q1. How long does a full bathroom renovation take in Dublin?**
A standard full bathroom typically runs seven to ten working days from strip-out to snag, and a more complex job — moving the layout, a wet-room build-up, or hidden damage found on strip-out — usually ten to fourteen. The honest figure depends on your room, and drying and curing times for levelling and tanking cannot be rushed without risking the finish. We give you a realistic programme with the written quote, not an optimistic promise.

**Q2. Do I need planning permission to renovate my bathroom?**
For a standard like-for-like renovation that does not extend the house or change how the space is used, the work is generally classed as exempted development, so no planning permission is required. Renovating within an existing bathroom, even with a new layout inside the same room, normally falls under this. Because every property differs, we confirm your specific situation rather than assume — and we will always tell you if something needs checking.

**Q3. Will you manage the plumber and electrician, or do I arrange them?**
We manage everything. That is the whole point of a turnkey renovation — one contractor accountable for the full project. Gerry coordinates the first and second-fix plumbing, the electrical work for your extract fan and RCD-protected circuits, the waterproofing, tiling and fit-out, all in the correct sequence. You have a single point of contact and one written quote, rather than juggling five trades and hoping they turn up in the right order.

**Q4. What does a full bathroom renovation include?**
It covers strip-out and skip disposal of the old suite and tiles, first-fix plumbing and electrics, any structural or substrate work such as floor stiffening and backer board, plastering and levelling, waterproofing the wet zones, tiling, second-fix, sanitaryware, shower screen and brassware, then grouting, sealing and a full snag. In short, everything from the empty shell to a finished room you can use — handled by one contractor.

**Q5. How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Dublin?**
Cost is shaped by factors, not a single figure, which is why we never post a fixed guaranteed price online. Keeping the existing layout keeps it down; moving the toilet or soil stack, discovering damp or rot on strip-out, a wet-room build-up, or large-format and natural-stone tiling push it up. We give a clear written quote after seeing the room. For real ranges and what drives them, see [what a bathroom renovation costs in Dublin](/cost-guide).

**Q6. What if you find damp or damage once the old bathroom is stripped out?**
Dublin's older houses often hide surprises behind the tiles — damp in a cold return, perished pipework, or a rotten section of subfloor. If we find anything, we stop, show you, and put the additional work in writing with a revised figure before continuing. Nothing gets buried or quietly added to the final bill. Tiling over active damp or a soft floor only guarantees the job fails, so we would never do it.

**Q7. Can you renovate a small ensuite as well as a main bathroom?**
Yes. Ensuite renovations follow the same turnkey process — strip-out, coordinated first and second fix, waterproofing, tiling and fit-out — just in a tighter space where careful setting-out and layout matter even more. Small rooms are less forgiving of poor planning, so signing off the tile layout and fixture positions before we start is where a compact ensuite is won. We give the same written quote and single point of contact.

**Q8. Do you handle the tiling yourselves or subcontract it?**
Tiling is Gerry's own trade — fifteen years of it — so the craft at the heart of your renovation is done by the person running the job, not handed to an unknown sub. That means the substrate prep, waterproofing and setting-out are all controlled by one accountable pair of hands. If your project needs specialist detail, you can see the full range across [all our tiling services](/services/tiling-services), all delivered to the same standard.
