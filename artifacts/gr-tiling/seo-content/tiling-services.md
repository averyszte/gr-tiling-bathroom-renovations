## INLINE PROSE SECTION 2

### How to choose a tiler in Dublin (and why prep matters)

The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest job. In Dublin's older housing stock the difference between a tile that lasts twenty years and one that cracks inside twelve months is almost always what happened *before* the first tile went on. Suspended timber floors flex and need stiffening and decoupling. Walls out of plumb by 20–30mm over a storey need levelling. Absorbent or dusty substrates need priming so the adhesive actually bonds. A tiler who skips these steps to win on price is quietly building in the failure. We'd rather explain the prep and quote it honestly than dazzle you with a low number that won't hold up.

When you're comparing tilers, look past the price line. Ask whether they're **fully insured** (we are), how long they've **actually been tiling** (15 years here), whether they'll show you **references or recent work**, and which **areas they cover** — we serve homes and businesses right across Dublin city and county. A good tiler will also set out the job from the most visible sightline rather than an out-of-square wall, plan the cuts so no sliver tiles land at eye level, and use flexible (S1/S2) adhesive over timber and heated floors. If those details never come up, that tells you something.

For anything wet, waterproofing is non-negotiable — tiles and cement grout are a wearing surface, not a waterproof one. We tank shower and wet-zone areas properly and detail the corners and pipe penetrations where most leaks actually start. The craft and technique behind that, plus adhesive and large-format guidance, lives on our [floor & wall tiling](/services/floor-wall-tiling-dublin) page.

**Checklist — questions to ask any Dublin tiler:**
- Are you fully insured, and can I see proof?
- How many years have you been tiling?
- Can you show references or recent Dublin jobs?
- How will you prep my floor/walls before tiling?
- Do you tank wet areas, and to what standard?
- Is the quote written down and fixed in scope?

## OPTIONAL DEEP SECTION

### Supply-and-fit vs fit-only — which is right for you?

Most Dublin tiling jobs come down to one early decision: do you want us to supply the tiles as well as fit them, or have you already got tiles you want laid? Both are completely normal, and the right choice depends on how much guidance you want and how confident you are in the material.

**Supply-and-fit** suits people who'd rather not gamble on the wrong tile. It's easy to fall for a beautiful floor tile that's actually rated R9 (fine dry, slippery wet) for a bathroom, or a soft, porous ceramic where dense porcelain belongs. When we supply, we match the tile to the room — slip rating, water absorption, format and the realities of the Irish climate — and there's a single point of responsibility for material and workmanship. It also smooths out ordering the right quantity, allowing for cuts, waste and a sensible attic-stock overage so a future repair matches.

**Fit-only** suits people who've already chosen — often something specific from a tile studio or an online supplier — and just want it laid properly. That's no problem at all. Our one ask is that you order enough: we'll tell you the coverage plus wastage before you buy, because patterned lays like herringbone and lots of cuts eat more tile than a straight lay. If tiles arrive short mid-job, a re-order can mean a different batch and a visible shade difference.

Whichever route you take, the fitting standard is identical and the quote is written down first. We don't publish fixed prices online because no honest tiler can price your walls and floors without seeing them — but we do give a clear written quote after a look, and for what drives cost up or down, see our [cost guide](/cost-guide).

**1. What areas of Dublin do you cover?**

**2. Do you supply the tiles or do I buy them?**
Both options work. With supply-and-fit we help you choose the right tile for the room, source it and lay it, with one point of responsibility for material and workmanship. With fit-only you buy the tiles you like and we fit them — we'll just tell you the coverage plus wastage first so you order enough. The fitting standard is identical either way.

**3. How much does tiling cost in Dublin?**
It depends on the area, the tile, the prep and the pattern — large-format and natural stone take longer, and herringbone or mosaic adds labour. Dublin labour also tends to run a little above the rest of Ireland. We don't post fixed prices online because no honest tiler can quote unseen, but we give a clear written quote after a visit. See our [cost guide](/cost-guide) for indicative ranges.

**4. Do you do both residential and commercial tiling?**
Yes. Most of our work is for Dublin homeowners — bathrooms, kitchens, hallways and full houses — but we also tile commercial spaces such as shops, cafés, offices and rental units. Commercial jobs often need harder-wearing porcelain, higher slip ratings underfoot and work scheduled around opening hours or a handover date. Tell us the setting and we'll spec and plan the job accordingly.

**5. Can you just fix or regrout my existing tiles?**
Often, yes — and it can save you a lot. If the tiles are sound and well bonded but the grout is stained, cracked or mouldy, raking out and regrouting plus renewing the silicone brings it back for a fraction of a re-tile. We only recommend replacing when tiles are drummy, lifting or cracked, or the waterproofing has failed. See our [tile & grout repairs](/services/tile-repairs-dublin) page for detail.

**6. My bathroom floor is a timber upstairs floor — can it be tiled?**
Yes, but it has to be done right. Suspended timber flexes, and flex is what cracks rigid tile and grout. We stiffen the deck where needed, add a decoupling membrane so board movement isn't transmitted into the tile, tank wet zones, and use a flexible (S1/S2) adhesive. No adhesive "rescues" a genuinely bouncy floor on its own — the prep does. Our [floor & wall tiling](/services/floor-wall-tiling-dublin) page covers the technique.

**7. Are you insured, and how experienced are you?**
Yes — GR Tiling is fully insured, Irish owned and operated, and Gerry has 15 years in the trade. We hold a 5.0 Google rating and are happy to provide references or point you to recent work. We'd always encourage you to check insurance and experience with any tiler you're considering; it's the simplest way to avoid a job that fails and costs more to put right later.
