## INLINE PROSE SECTION 1

### Bathroom tiling in Dublin, done to last

A bathroom is the hardest room in the house to tile well. It gets wet every day, it moves, and it shows every mistake under bright light. Getting **bathroom tiling in Dublin** right is less about laying tile and more about everything that happens before the first tile is fixed — sound substrate, proper waterproofing, and setting-out that lands full tiles where the eye falls. Skip those and even beautiful tiles fail within a couple of years.

Gerry has spent 15 years tiling bathrooms across Dublin, from Victorian returns in Rathmines and Ranelagh to 1970s semis in Raheny and new-build ensuites in the docklands. Each throws up its own problems. Older homes hide lath-and-plaster, walls out of plumb by 20–30mm over a storey, and bouncy timber floors upstairs. Newer builds have stud walls and floating decks that still move. We survey what is actually behind the old tiles rather than assuming, because a lasting job depends on knowing the truth of the substrate. If you want the detail on how we treat different backgrounds, see [how we prep floors & walls](/services/floor-wall-tiling-dublin).

The wet zone is where we concentrate. Tiles and cement grout are **not waterproof** — grout is porous and every joint is a path for water. In a shower, water that gets behind the tile has nowhere to go: it rots timber, corrodes fixings, blows plaster and leaks into the room below. That is why we tank the wet area before tiling, band every internal corner and pipe penetration, and seal around the shower waste. Most shower leaks are a detailing failure at a junction, not the flat field.

- Tap-test and inspect the existing substrate before quoting — no guesswork
- Tank shower walls and floors with banded corners and sealed penetrations
- Backer board over timber and stud rather than tiling straight onto plasterboard
- Set out from the most visible line so no sliver tiles land at eye level
- Silicone — not grout — at every movement junction and where tile meets sanitaryware
- Left clean and tidy each day, on time and on the agreed written quote

## INLINE PROSE SECTION 2

### Showers, ensuites and the details that matter

Most of our calls are shower enclosures over a tray and compact ensuites — the two jobs where craft counts most. A shower enclosure needs the walls tanked, the tray bedded and sealed, and the tile set out so the visible reveals are balanced rather than a fat cut on one side and a sliver on the other. On a **shower floor**, we favour small-format tile or mosaic: the extra grout lines add grip underfoot and let the floor fall correctly to the waste. For slip resistance we spec sensibly — an R10 surface suits a bathroom used with a mat, while a walk-in shower floor wants R11 or better.

Ensuites and small Dublin bathrooms are unforgiving because there is nowhere to hide a mistake — every wall is in view at once. Good setting-out is the whole game: we plan the tile layout, agree it with you before anything is fixed, and cut around the WC, basin and screen so the pattern reads cleanly. **Tiled niches** are a favourite detail — a recessed shelf in the shower wall for bottles, tanked and tiled so it drains and never traps water behind it. Done well it looks built-in; done badly it is the first thing to leak.

For a full trayless, level-floor room with a former and graded falls, that is a different build — see our [full wet room installation](/services/wet-room-installation-dublin). And if your existing tiles are sound but the grout is stained or the silicone is peeling, you may not need retiling at all; our [tile & grout repairs](/services/tile-repairs-dublin) can refresh it for far less. For a sense of what a project runs to, the [bathroom tiling costs](/cost-guide) guide sets out the ranges honestly.

## OPTIONAL DEEP SECTION

### Waterproofing before tiling: why it is the part you can't see that keeps a Dublin bathroom dry

Ask any honest tiler where showers fail and the answer is almost never the tile — it is the waterproofing behind it, or the lack of it. British Standard BS 5385, the tiling trade's reference, is explicit that the tiled finish is a wearing surface, not the waterproof layer. In Dublin's damp, often poorly ventilated housing stock, tanking the wet zone is not an optional upgrade; it is the difference between a bathroom that lasts fifteen years and one that quietly rots the joists beneath it.

We tank using either a liquid-applied membrane — primer plus a flexible waterproof coating, with waterproof tape banded into every internal corner and around each pipe — or a bonded sheet membrane, depending on the room. Over a suspended timber shower floor, which is the highest-risk detail in domestic tiling, we use a decoupling membrane that is also waterproof: it separates the tile from a moving deck so board movement is not transmitted into the tile, and forms part of the tank at the same time. The failures we are called to fix are almost always the predictable ones — unbanded floor-to-wall junctions, no seal at the shower waste, membrane stopped too low up the wall, incompatible products mixed between systems, or tiling before the membrane had cured.

Substrate comes first, though, because no membrane and no adhesive rescues a floor that flexes. A bouncy upstairs timber floor — common in Victorian and Edwardian terraces where joists are undersized and notched by generations of plumbers — must be stiffened and decoupled before tiling, or rigid tile and grout will crack. We over-board or add noggins where needed, prime absorbent backgrounds so the adhesive bonds rather than drying out, and level floors that are out by more than a few millimetres so large-format tiles don't lip. It is slower and it costs more upfront than a cheap quote that skips it — but skipping tanking, priming and movement joints is exactly why the cheap quote fails. We give you an honest written quote for the job done properly, never a fixed guaranteed price plucked off a screen.

## FAQ (6-8 Q&A)

**1. Do you waterproof the shower before tiling, or just tile over the walls?**
We always tank the wet zone before any tile goes on. Tiles and cement grout are porous, so water will get behind them — the waterproof membrane is what catches it. We band every internal corner, seal around pipes and the shower waste, and take the membrane to a sensible height. Over timber floors we use a decoupling membrane that is also waterproof. This is standard BS 5385 practice, not an extra.

**2. How long does it take to tile a bathroom or ensuite in Dublin?**
A straightforward shower enclosure or small ensuite is usually a few days on the tiling and waterproofing alone; a full bathroom with floor and walls runs longer, and the tanking must fully cure before grouting. Awkward access, natural stone, intricate patterns and older substrates that need levelling all add time. We give you a realistic schedule in your written quote rather than a rushed promise, and we turn up when we say we will.

**3. Can you tile over my existing tiles or old bathroom walls?**
Sometimes, but we never assume. We tap-test for hollow or drummy tiles and check the wall is sound, flat and dry first. Tiling over loose tiles, active damp or a moving substrate just guarantees the new work fails too. Often the honest answer in an older Dublin home is to strip back to a sound background and board it — we will tell you which applies and price it straight.

**4. What tiles work best on a shower floor?**
Small-format tiles or mosaics. The extra grout lines give grip underfoot when the floor is wet and let the surface fall neatly to the drain, which large tiles can't do on a graded floor. For slip resistance we look for around R11 on a walk-in shower floor. Porcelain is our default for wet areas — dense, low water absorption and hard-wearing in the Irish climate. We will talk you through the options for your room.

**5. My bathroom is tiny — will the tiles still look right?**
Small rooms are where setting-out matters most, because every wall is in view at once. We plan the layout so full tiles land where the eye falls and cuts sit in the least visible places — never a sliver at eye level. We set out from the most prominent line rather than an out-of-square wall, and agree the plan with you before fixing. Done right, careful setting-out makes a compact ensuite feel considered rather than cramped.

**6. Can you build a tiled niche or shelf into the shower?**
Yes, and it is one of the nicer details we do. A recessed niche gives you somewhere for bottles without a plastic caddy. The key is that it is tanked and tiled to drain, with sealed junctions, so water never sits behind it — a badly built niche is the first thing to leak. We set its position and height into your tile layout so the surrounding courses stay clean rather than being cut around an afterthought.

**7. Do I need a full wet room, or will a tiled shower enclosure do?**
A tiled enclosure over a tray suits most Dublin bathrooms and is what we do most. A full wet room is a trayless, level-floor build with a former and graded falls — more work, more waterproofing, and a different price bracket. If that is what you are after, see our full wet room installation page. If you just want a well-tiled, properly tanked shower, an enclosure is usually the sensible, cost-effective choice.

**8. What does bathroom tiling cost in Dublin?**
It depends on the room — size, tile format, how much prep and levelling the substrate needs, whether it is a simple splashback or a fully tanked shower, and pattern complexity. Large-format and natural stone are slower and more skilled; herringbone and mosaics add labour. We don't post fixed prices because every bathroom is different, but our cost guide sets out honest ranges, and we always give you a clear written quote after seeing the job.
