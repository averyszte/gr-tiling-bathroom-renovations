## INLINE PROSE SECTION 2

### Heading: What a proper bathroom quote actually covers

Understanding what sits inside a quote is the best way to compare two of them fairly. A full refit quote should account for strip-out and skip disposal, any substrate repair and preparation, first-fix plumbing and electrical coordination, waterproofing in the wet zones, the tiling itself, the sanitaryware and brassware, and the finishing and making-good at the end. When one quote is dramatically lighter than another, it is almost never because that tiler is faster — it is because something on that list has quietly been left out.

The part most quotes hide is preparation, and it is exactly where the cheapest quote cuts corners. Tiling straight onto bare plasterboard, an un-primed floor, or a bouncy timber deck is how you get cracked grout and drummy tiles within a year. Proper prep — priming absorbent surfaces, fitting tile backer boards, self-levelling an uneven floor, using a flexible adhesive over timber, and building in movement joints — is work you are right to pay for, because it is what makes the finish last. That craft is explained in full on [our tiling services](/services/tiling-services) page. Here, just know that the invisible work is the work that decides whether you are re-doing the room in three years.

- **Strip-out and disposal** — more than a partial refresh, less than you think if the base is sound
- **Substrate preparation** — priming, backer boards, levelling, decoupling where needed
- **Waterproofing** — essential in showers and wet rooms, never a line to remove
- **Tiling labour** — the biggest craft line, and where skill genuinely shows
- **Sanitaryware and brassware** — the part your own taste controls
- **Making good** — the tidy, finished result you actually live with

## OPTIONAL DEEP SECTION

### Heading: What actually drives a bathroom quote up or down

Two bathrooms of identical size on the same Dublin road can quote very differently, and it is almost never the taps. Understanding the real cost drivers is the difference between a budget that holds and one that does not. Here is the honest picture of what moves the number — no figures, because the figures are yours to receive in writing, but a clear sense of which way each factor pushes.

**What pushes a quote up.** A full strip-out and skip disposal costs more than a partial refresh. Older Dublin housing stock is the biggest variable of all: the Victorian and Edwardian terraces of Rathmines, Ranelagh and Phibsborough, and the coastal villages, sit on suspended timber floors that flex and must be stiffened, over-boarded and decoupled before a single tile goes down — you cannot lay rigid tile on a bouncy deck and expect it to survive. Walls that are noticeably out of plumb need levelling. Hidden damp or rot discovered on strip-out has to be fixed, not tiled over. A wet room build-up with full-room tanking, a former and graded falls is more involved than a simple shower enclosure — our [wet room installation](/services/wet-room-installation-dublin) page explains why that detail is where corners must never be cut. Then come the finish choices: large-format or natural stone (slower, more skilled, more waste), underfloor heating, moving the soil stack, premium sanitaryware, and period houses with narrow stairs and no parking.

**What keeps a quote down.** A sound existing substrate that needs no structural work. Keeping the layout so no plumbing moves and no walls get chased. Standard-format porcelain in a simple straight lay. A like-for-like refit rather than a reconfiguration. If your existing floor and walls are solid and you are happy with where things sit, you are already at the lighter end of the scale.

The uncomfortable truth behind every cost guide is this: skill and correct materials cost a little more upfront and far less over the life of the bathroom. The cheapest quote in your inbox is usually cheapest because it has quietly skipped tanking, priming, movement joints or grab-rail pattressing — the invisible work that does not show on day one but decides whether you are re-doing the room in three years. A slightly higher honest quote that includes all of it is the cheaper bathroom in the end. That is the whole reason we quote in writing after seeing the room, and never post a fixed guaranteed price we would have to inflate to protect ourselves.

## FAQ

**How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Dublin?**
We don't publish a figure, and we would be suspicious of anyone who does without seeing your room. The cost is shaped by the state of your subfloor and walls, whether the layout stays put, the condition hidden behind the old tiles, and the finishes you choose. Rather than a phone guess we would only have to revise, Gerry surveys the actual bathroom and gives you an itemised written quote you can rely on. This page explains every factor that moves the number.

**What does it cost to tile a bathroom in Ireland?**
It depends on real, measurable things: the area being tiled, whether it is a straightforward straight lay or an intricate pattern like herringbone, the tile format (large-format and natural stone take more skill and time), and how much preparation the surface needs first. Because those vary job to job, we give a written quote after seeing the space rather than a fixed online rate. Preparation is always part of it and never the corner to cut.

**Why won't you give me a fixed price over the phone?**
Because no honest tradesman can quote your bathroom without seeing it. The biggest cost drivers are hidden — the state of the subfloor, damp behind the tiles, whether the walls are true — and none of that shows in a phone call. A cheap phone figure only gets protected later with change orders. Instead, Gerry surveys the actual room and gives you an itemised written quote you can trust, so the agreed price is the price you pay.

**Why is the cheapest quote often a false economy?**
The cheapest quote is usually cheapest because it has quietly removed the invisible work — tanking in wet zones, priming absorbent surfaces, backer boards, movement joints, and timber pattressing behind grab rails. None of that shows on day one, but skipping it is exactly why a bathroom cracks, leaks or goes mouldy within a couple of years. A slightly higher quote that includes proper prep and waterproofing is genuinely the cheaper bathroom over its life.

**Why does a wet room cost more than a standard shower?**
Because it is a more involved build. A wet room needs a drain, a graded screed or former so the floor falls correctly, and full-room tanking rather than a simple tray. How much more it comes to depends heavily on your floor — especially over suspended timber in older Dublin homes — which is why it can only be quoted after a survey. The waterproofing is the one line you must never cut, since a failed tank can cause damage many times its cost. See our wet room installation page for how that build is done properly.

**Is regrouting cheaper than retiling?**
Yes, considerably — when your tiles are sound and well-bonded and only the grout and silicone have failed, regrouting restores the surface for a fraction of the disruption of a retile. Retiling is only necessary when tiles are drummy, lifting or cracked, or when the waterproofing behind them has failed. We give you the honest verdict on which your bathroom actually needs, and quote it in writing. Our [tile & grout repairs](/services/tile-repairs-dublin) page explains the difference.

**Should I budget a contingency, and how much?**
Yes — a sensible contingency is wise on almost any Dublin bathroom, and more so in older housing stock. Victorian and Edwardian terraces and mid-century semis regularly hide surprises on strip-out: rotten timber under a leaking tray, bridged damp, or a subfloor that needs stiffening. A contingency means an honest surprise does not derail your project. We show you any genuine issue and price it openly before doing the work — it is never a blank cheque.

**Does moving the bath or toilet add much to the cost?**
It does — relocating fixtures means new first-fix plumbing, possibly moving the soil stack, and chasing walls and floors that then need making good. Keeping your existing layout is the single biggest saving available on any refit. If your current arrangement works, we will usually advise keeping it and spending the budget on finishes and proper prep instead. We will always tell you honestly where the money is best spent.
