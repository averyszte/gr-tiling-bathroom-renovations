## INLINE PROSE SECTION 1

### Heading: The affordable fix before the expensive one

Most Dublin bathrooms don't need ripping out — they need the joints sorted. Grout is porous by nature and silicone perishes, so after eight to ten years the lines go dark, crack and let water track behind the tile. Left alone, that moisture rots timber, blows plaster and shows up as a stain on the ceiling below. Caught early, it's a straightforward repair: we rake out the tired grout, renew it, cut out and replace the silicone at every movement junction, and your bathroom looks and performs like new for a fraction of a refit.

The honest question we answer on every visit is regrout or retile. If your tiles are sound and well-bonded and only the grout and silicone have failed, regrouting is the right call — quicker, cleaner and far cheaper. If tiles are drummy, lifting or the bed beneath them is moving, fresh grout on a failing base just fails again, and we'll tell you that straight. For those bigger jobs we'll point you toward full bathroom re-tiling rather than sell you a repair that won't hold. That honesty is why so much of Gerry's work in Dublin comes by word of mouth.

Because this is close, wet-zone work in older housing stock, the detail matters more than the drama. Dublin's Victorian returns, 1950s semis and newer apartments all move differently, and the failure is almost always at a junction — a corner, a floor-to-wall line, the edge of a bath — not out in the flat field of tile. Get the junctions right with the correct materials and the repair lasts.

**Checklist — what a proper repair includes:**
- Raking grout out to a sound depth, not just skimming the surface
- Sanitary-grade anti-mould silicone at every movement junction
- Old silicone fully removed — never a fresh bead over perished old
- Mould treated at the source, not painted over
- Porous grout and any natural stone resealed to slow re-staining
- An honest verdict on whether repair or retile is the sensible spend

## INLINE PROSE SECTION 2

### Heading: Grout, silicone and where each belongs

People use "grout" and "silicone" interchangeably, but they do different jobs, and mixing them up is why so many DIY repairs fail within months. Grout is rigid and fills the joints between tiles across the flat field. Silicone is flexible and belongs only at the movement junctions — floor-to-wall, internal corners, around the bath, tray and basin, and wherever tile meets sanitaryware. Put rigid grout where the building moves and it cracks; that's the thin dark line that keeps reappearing no matter how often it's filled.

Discolouration is a separate story from cracking. Cement grout absorbs soap, limescale, dirt and mould spores, so it darkens over time even when it's structurally fine — that's a cleaning and resealing job, not a rip-out. Cracking, by contrast, means movement or voids under the tile, and it needs the joint raked out and renewed with the right product. We assess which you've actually got before quoting, because there's no sense charging you for a regrout when a deep clean and reseal would do. For a shower a client wants to keep pristine, we'll talk through epoxy grout — near-impervious and stain-resistant — versus standard cementitious grout that's cheaper but needs sealing and eventual renewal.

Silicone that's gone black, shrunken or peeling can't be rescued. Fresh silicone over old never bonds and traps the mould underneath, so we cut it out completely, treat the junction and lay a clean bead of anti-mould sanitary silicone. If the mould keeps coming back after a proper renewal, that's often a ventilation issue behind the tiles worth investigating rather than a sealant you can keep replacing.

**Checklist — signs it's time to call:**
- Grout lines cracked, crumbling or missing in patches
- Black or pink mould in the silicone that won't scrub out
- Silicone lifting, shrinking or peeling away from the tile
- Tiles that sound hollow (drummy) when tapped, or feel loose
- Water appearing below a bathroom, or a musty damp smell
- Grout gone permanently dark despite regular cleaning

## OPTIONAL DEEP SECTION

### Heading: Regrout or retile? The tap test decides

The single most useful thing a tiler does on a repair call is the tap test, and it settles the regrout-versus-retile question before a cent is spent. We run a knuckle or a light tool across the tiled area and listen. A solid, dull note means the tile is well-bonded to the substrate — the bed is sound, and if only the grout and silicone have failed, a regrout restores the whole surface at a fraction of a retile. A hollow, drummy note means there's a void or the tile has debonded. One or two drummy tiles can often be lifted and replaced individually; a whole wall or floor sounding hollow means the bed has failed, and no amount of new grout will fix that.

Grout is the honest early-warning system for what's happening behind it. When it cracks repeatedly along the same line, the tiles either side are usually moving — a flexing floor, a missing movement joint, or a substrate that was never stiff enough. In Dublin's older houses that's common on suspended timber upper floors, where decades of plumbers have notched and drilled the joists and the deck now flexes underfoot. Regrouting a floor like that buys you months, not years, unless the deck is stiffened first. Being upfront about that is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that brings us back.

There's also a line where repair stops making sense and the money is better spent once, properly. If the waterproofing behind a shower has failed — water getting past the tanking, not just discolouring the grout — the fix is a wet room re-waterproofing, not a regrout, because the leak is behind the tile where grout can't reach. Likewise a bathroom floor on a failed substrate wants proper floor re-tiling rather than a patch. We'll always tell you which side of that line your bathroom sits on, and if it's borderline we'll give you both options in writing so you decide. If you're weighing up cost, our regrout & repair costs guide sets out the factors that move the price, and you can see our full tiling services if the job turns out bigger than a repair.

For most Dublin bathrooms, though, the good news holds: sound tiles, tired joints, and a same-week fix that makes the room feel renovated without the renovation.

## FAQ (7 Q&A)

**Q1: Can you replace grout without removing the tiles?**
Yes — that's the whole point of a regrout. As long as the tiles are sound and well-bonded, we rake the old grout out of the joints to a proper depth and fill them with fresh grout, leaving the tiles in place. It restores the look and seals the surface again at a fraction of a retile. We only need to lift tiles when they're cracked, loose or drummy, and even then it's usually just the affected ones.

**Q2: How do I know if I need a regrout or a full retile?**
We tap the tiles and listen. A solid, dull sound means the tile is well-bonded and a regrout will do. A hollow, drummy sound means the bed has failed and new grout won't hold — that needs tiles lifted or a full re-tile. Cracked, lifting or moving tiles, or failed waterproofing, all point to a bigger job. We give you the honest verdict in writing so you're not paying for a repair that won't last.

**Q3: How often should grout be replaced?**
Cement grout typically needs renewing around every eight to ten years, sooner if it's cracked, crumbling or has gone permanently dark. It's porous, so it absorbs soap, limescale and mould over time. Regular resealing slows that down, but grout is a wearing surface and eventually needs raking out and replacing. If yours is cracking well before that, it usually points to movement underneath rather than age, which we'd check on the visit.

**Q4: Why does mould keep coming back on my silicone?**
Because fresh silicone laid over old never bonds properly and traps mould underneath, so it grows straight back through. The only lasting fix is to cut the old silicone out completely, treat the junction and lay a new bead of sanitary anti-mould silicone. If it still returns after a proper renewal, the cause is usually poor ventilation or moisture getting behind the tiles — worth investigating rather than replacing the sealant on repeat.

**Q5: What does tile and grout repair cost in Dublin?**
It depends on the size of the area, whether it's a regrout, a silicone renewal, a few loose tiles or a combination, and how the existing joints come out. Small silicone and mould jobs are low-ticket; a full shower regrout is more. We never quote a fixed price online — we give an honest written quote after seeing the job. Our regrout & repair costs guide explains the factors that move the price up or down.

**Q6: My tiles look fine but water is getting through — can you just regrout it?**
Sometimes, but not always. If the grout has failed and water is tracking through the joints, a regrout and silicone renewal seals it. But if the waterproofing behind a shower has failed, the leak is behind the tile where grout can't reach, and regrouting only masks it. We'll check which it is. A true waterproofing failure needs wet room re-waterproofing done properly, and we'd tell you that rather than sell you a patch.

**Q7: Do you reseal natural stone and porous grout?**
Yes. Natural stone — marble, limestone, travertine — and cement grout are both porous and stain or etch if left unsealed. Resealing periodically is normal maintenance, not a sign anything's wrong, and it keeps the surface shedding water and resisting soap and limescale. We can reseal as a standalone job or as the finishing step after a regrout, and we'll tell you honestly how often your particular surface will need it.
